# SILT Core positioning

SILT Core defines a semantic layer for moments of encounter between participants and systems grounded in different legal, customary, cultural, private, institutional or technical orders.

Its canonical seam is:

> **Source -> Standing -> Presentation -> evaluation at the encounter**

SILT does not attempt to replace identity, credential, authorisation, governance, payments or execution systems. It asks a different question: what relational legitimacy is being brought into this encounter, from what Source, under what conditions, and what may safely be concluded from the available Presentation and Evidence?

## Layering

Identity and credential systems may provide Evidence. Capability, authorisation and agent-control systems may carry, constrain or enforce operational permissions. Governance frameworks may define rules, roles and institutional processes. Legal-context systems may publish terms or downstream effects. None of those functions is universally equivalent to SILT Standing or Authority.

SILT is therefore best treated as a semantic layer that can sit across or above those mechanisms without requiring them to adopt one legal or cultural ontology.

## Boundary

SILT is semantically thick and operationally thin. Authentication, key continuity, session management, capability tokens, dynamic authorisation, access control and runtime execution remain outside Core unless an implementation demonstrates a semantic distinction that cannot otherwise be expressed.

The aim is mutual legibility without semantic surrender. Conformance to SILT does not manufacture the legitimacy of an underlying Source, Standing, Authority or Profile Expression.
