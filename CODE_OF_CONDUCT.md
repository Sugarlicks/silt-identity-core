# SILT Code of Conduct

SILT is a standards, research and implementation project concerned with encounters between different legal, customary, cultural, private, institutional and technical orders. Participation therefore requires more than ordinary technical civility: contributors must take care not to erase, appropriate or misrepresent the authority, knowledge or position of others.

## Expected conduct

Participants are expected to:

- engage respectfully, including in strong technical, legal or conceptual disagreement;
- criticise ideas, claims, designs and evidence rather than attacking people;
- distinguish clearly between personal interpretation, community position, institutional mandate and established source material;
- avoid presenting another person, community, organisation, legal order or cultural tradition as endorsing a position without an adequate basis for doing so;
- treat Indigenous, customary and community knowledge with particular care, including where material is contextual, restricted, collectively held or not appropriately abstracted into a technical representation;
- respect requests to correct misattribution, overstatement of authority or inappropriate disclosure;
- minimise unnecessary disclosure of personal, relational or culturally sensitive information;
- disclose material conflicts of interest where they are relevant to a contribution or decision.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following is not acceptable in project spaces:

- harassment, intimidation, threats or sustained personal abuse;
- discriminatory or demeaning conduct based on identity, background, culture, nationality, ethnicity, religion, gender, disability, sexual orientation or similar characteristics;
- deliberate misrepresentation of another contributor's position or authority;
- knowingly claiming community, institutional, legal or cultural endorsement that has not been given;
- publishing private, confidential, restricted or culturally sensitive material without an appropriate basis;
- retaliating against a person for raising a good-faith concern;
- using participation in SILT to pressure others into disclosing more identity, Source material or relational information than is necessary for the work at hand.

## Disagreement is not misconduct

SILT deals with subjects on which reasonable people, institutions and legal or cultural orders may disagree profoundly. Disagreement over law, governance, authority, standards, politics, history, culture or technical design is not itself a breach of this Code.

Participants may challenge claims directly and robustly. The line is crossed when disagreement becomes harassment, discrimination, deliberate misrepresentation, coercion or other conduct described above.

## Knowledge, standing and authority claims

Because SILT is specifically concerned with Standing, Authority, Source and plural orders, contributors should be precise about the basis on which they speak.

No contributor should assume that identity, membership, credentials, professional status, technical control or participation in a discussion automatically establishes authority to speak for a collective, community, institution or normative order.

Where a contribution depends on such authority, the relevant basis should be stated as far as is appropriate without forcing unnecessary disclosure.

## Scope

This Code applies to SILT project spaces, including repository issues, pull requests, discussions, review processes, working sessions and project-related communications where a participant is acting in a SILT capacity.

## Reporting

Concerns should be raised with the project maintainers. Do not post sensitive details publicly if doing so could expose or further harm an affected person or community.

Where no private reporting channel is yet published, open a minimal issue requesting private maintainer contact without disclosing the substance of the concern.

Reports should identify the conduct at issue, the relevant context and any immediate safety or confidentiality concerns. Anonymous or third-party reports may be considered where there is enough information to assess them fairly.

## Response and enforcement

Maintainers may respond by seeking clarification, requesting correction, moderating or removing material, restricting participation, or taking other proportionate action.

Responses should be based on the conduct and available evidence rather than status or seniority. Where a maintainer is materially involved in the matter, they should not be the sole decision-maker.

Serious or repeated breaches may result in temporary or permanent exclusion from project spaces.

The purpose of enforcement is to protect the integrity of the project and the people and communities engaging with it, not to suppress legitimate disagreement.

## Project principle

SILT's own architecture depends on preserving difference without forcing collapse into a single frame. The same discipline applies to participation in the project: make positions legible, challenge them where necessary, and do not manufacture authority that is not yours.
