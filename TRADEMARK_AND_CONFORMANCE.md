# SILT - Interim Trademark and Conformance Claim Policy

**Status:** Interim policy for v0.2. No formal SILT certification programme is yet in operation.

## 1. Purpose

Open use of the SILT semantic grammar should not be confused with permission to imply endorsement, certification or authority over the SILT marks.

The specification licences grant copyright/software rights only. They do not grant trademark or certification rights.

## 2. Descriptive use

Factual, referential language may be used to describe an implementation or document, for example:

- "implements SILT Core v0.2";
- "uses the SILT Core v0.2 semantic model";
- "tested against the SILT Core v0.2 conformance suite".

Such wording must not imply sponsorship, certification or endorsement where none exists.

## 3. Self-assessed conformance

Until a formal certification programme exists, a party may make a factual self-assessed conformance claim only if it identifies the exact specification and conformance-suite version used and does not present the result as project-issued certification.

Preferred form:

> Self-assessed for conformance with SILT Core v0.2 using [suite version/hash]. This is not SILT certification.

A conformance claim is about preservation of the published semantic grammar. It does not establish the underlying legal, customary, cultural, institutional or relational legitimacy of any Source, Standing, Authority or Profile Expression.

## 4. Reserved claims and marks

The following should not be used as certification or endorsement marks without written permission or a future published certification policy:

- `SILT Certified`;
- `SILT-compatible` when presented as an official badge or assurance mark;
- SILT logos or visual marks in a way that implies endorsement;
- any wording likely to imply that the SILT project has certified substantive legal or cultural legitimacy.

This interim reservation does not prevent ordinary descriptive reference to SILT or discussion of compatibility in prose, provided the context does not suggest official certification.

## 5. Future certification

A future assurance programme may test implementation conformance, profile handling, semantic lineage or other published criteria. It must preserve the rule that technical conformance does not manufacture legitimacy.

Any certification programme should publish its test basis, scope, version, revocation process and appeal or correction mechanism before issuing marks.

## 6. Logos and brand assets

Unless a specific asset states otherwise, SILT logos and brand assets are not licensed under CC BY 4.0 or Apache License 2.0 merely because they appear near licensed specification or code material.
