# SILT Core v0.2.0 — Semantic Architecture for Plural Encounters

**Draft status:** reviewer-candidate release text. Not yet published as a GitHub release.

SILT Core v0.2.0 is a substantial architectural revision rather than an incremental schema update.

It defines SILT Core as a semantic architecture through which participants, collectives and systems operating under different legal, customary, cultural, private, institutional or technical orders can become mutually legible at moments of encounter without requiring those orders to collapse into a single ontology or surrender their own sources of authority.

The canonical architectural seam is:

> **Source → Standing → Presentation → evaluation at the encounter**

The governing design posture is:

> **semantically thick, operationally thin**

## Why v0.2

Identity, credential, authentication and authorisation infrastructure can establish important technical facts: who controls a key, what credential verifies, what account has permission, what capability can execute, or whether a policy engine returns an allow decision.

Those facts do not necessarily establish the semantic relation that makes an action legitimate in a particular encounter.

SILT Core v0.2 separates those layers. It preserves the meaning of Source-grounded Standing, Authority and related relations while leaving authentication, key continuity, credentials, capability tokens, dynamic authorisation, access control and runtime execution to downstream systems.

Technical success does not by itself establish Standing or Authority.

## Architectural changes from v0.1

v0.2 introduces or substantially develops:

- **Source** as the referenced ground or provenance from which Standing, Authority, Obligation or another semantic relation derives its claimed meaning;
- **Standing** as a relational, Source-grounded position rather than a portable status or credential-backed property;
- **Presentation** as the encounter-specific act or envelope through which a Participant brings the minimum relevant projection of Standing or another semantic claim into an encounter;
- **Profile Expression** as a bounded, selective and non-exhaustive expression of encounter-relevant semantic conditions;
- **Obligation** as a persistent relational state distinct from Authority;
- **Technical Capability** as operational ability distinct from semantic Authority;
- **semantic continuity** as distinct from cryptographic continuity;
- Profile-Expression-specific evaluation using `SATISFIED`, `NOT_SATISFIED` and `INDETERMINATE`;
- explicit support for plural, conflicting or contested Profile Expressions without a universal precedence or merger rule;
- **deliberate non-expression** where reducing a condition to bounded expression would materially distort it;
- explicit separation between collective Standing and Authority to represent, bind, speak for or disclose on behalf of a collective;
- **Attribution** as distinct from technical execution, Binding and liability.

v0.2 removes from universal Core:

- `Status`;
- universal `Capacity`;
- `Principal` as the foundational subject model, replaced by thin `Participant`;
- the older `Lex/Profile` split, replaced by **Profile Expression**;
- Delegation as a standalone universal Core primitive, with delegation instead represented as an Action through which derived Authority may be constituted where applicable;
- universal `ALLOW` / `DENY` as Core semantic outcomes;
- any implication that successful SILT evaluation automatically produces Binding, liability, enforceability or compulsory recognition.

## Evaluation at the encounter

SILT evaluates bounded semantic conditions for a particular encounter.

A receiving Participant or order may conclude that its conditions are not satisfied without thereby creating, extinguishing or redefining the underlying Source-grounded Standing.

Where several Profile Expressions apply, results remain linked to the expression under which they were produced. SILT Core does not silently merge, rank or privilege conflicting expressions.

Where the semantic state is insufficient for a conclusion, the result may be `INDETERMINATE` rather than an invented rule.

Where faithful bounded expression itself would materially distort a condition, SILT does not require that condition to enter evaluation at all. Deliberate non-expression is not a fourth evaluation outcome.

## Pre-freeze pressure testing

Before semantic freeze, the v0.2 architecture was tested against four deliberately different worked encounters:

- **WE01 — Transferable Instrument Encounter:** changing relational Standing, persistent Obligation, technical control, constitutive transfer and incomplete lineage;
- **WE02 — Credential-Carried Institutional Encounter:** credential Evidence, current Standing, Authority, key rotation, conflicting credentials and selective disclosure;
- **WE03 — Plural / Collective Encounter:** conflicting Profile Expressions, collective Standing, representational Authority, contested expression provenance and deliberate non-expression;
- **WE04 — Recursive AI Delegation Encounter:** derived Authority, scope containment, semantic lineage, Revocation, Attribution and technically successful execution without semantic Authority.

Across those tests, the machine-readable conformance suite and an experimental adjacent-protocol mapping, no recurring unnamed semantic distinction was identified that required a new Core primitive.

That result supports the v0.2 freeze. It is not a claim that SILT is complete for every future domain.

## Migration from v0.1

v0.2 supersedes v0.1 as the controlling SILT Core semantic architecture for new Core work once the `v0.2.0` tag is published.

It does not retroactively rewrite implementations that began against v0.1. In particular, the Vietsch / AUT CISRC research-delegation implementation remains on its v0.1 semantic basis through completion; any later migration or compatibility assessment is separate work.

See the [v0.1 → v0.2 repository migration and reconciliation record](../migration/v0.1-to-v0.2-repository-audit.md) and the full [CHANGELOG](../../CHANGELOG.md).

## What SILT Core still does not do

v0.2 does not define a universal identity model, legal ontology, verifier, authorisation engine, conflict-of-laws system or law engine.

It does not determine universal legal validity, compel a receiving order to recognise a Presentation, certify cultural or collective belonging, or resolve incompatible normative orders into one superior frame.

Authentication, credential transport, keys, capabilities, dynamic authorisation, policy execution and runtime enforcement remain downstream operational concerns.

SILT Core supplies a grammar for preserving relevant semantic distinctions when those systems meet.
