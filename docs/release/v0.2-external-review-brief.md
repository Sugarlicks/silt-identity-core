# SILT Core v0.2 — External Review Brief

**Status:** reviewer package for Freeze Candidate 1  
**Branch:** `release/v0.2-fc1`  
**Review stage:** final semantic / release-claim review before merge, tag and DOI publication

Thank you for reviewing SILT Core v0.2 at the final pre-release stage.

This is a **bounded release review**, not an invitation to redesign SILT from first principles. The current architecture has already been through an extended internal design and adversarial testing process. We are asking for an independent judgement on whether the release candidate contains a genuine semantic defect, contradiction, accidental ontology capture, or public claim that exceeds what the architecture actually establishes.

The canonical seam is:

> **Source → Standing → Presentation → evaluation at the encounter**

The governing design posture is:

> **semantically thick, operationally thin**

The central claim is that legal, customary, cultural, private, institutional and technical orders can become mutually legible at moments of encounter without being required to collapse into one ontology or surrender their own Sources of authority.

## 1. What we are asking you to decide

Please answer the following primary question:

> **Is there any release-blocking semantic reason SILT Core v0.2 should not be frozen and published in its present architecture?**

A release blocker would include, for example:

- a contradiction between settled Core concepts;
- a missing semantic distinction that repeatedly has to be smuggled in through informal convention;
- an architectural move that silently privileges one legal, cultural, institutional or technical ontology as universal;
- a collapse of Evidence into Source, Technical Capability into Authority, identity into Standing, or evaluation into Binding;
- an inconsistency between the canonical architecture and the WE01–WE04 pressure tests;
- release language that materially claims more than the architecture supports.

Please do **not** treat every possible future enhancement, implementation feature or adjacent-standard mapping as a release blocker.

## 2. Settled architecture under review

The following are intentional v0.2 decisions. They should be challenged if internally incoherent, but not merely because an alternative architecture is possible.

- **Standing is relational and grounded in Source.** It is not a universal credential, status or self-certified property.
- **Presentation is encounter-specific.** It brings a bounded, purpose-relevant projection into an encounter; it does not create the underlying Standing.
- **Profile Expression is bounded and non-exhaustive.** It supplies encounter-relevant conditions without becoming a complete representation of the originating order or a second architectural spine.
- **Evaluation is encounter- and Profile-Expression-specific.** The Core outcomes are `SATISFIED`, `NOT_SATISFIED` and `INDETERMINATE`.
- **Deliberate non-expression is not a fourth outcome.** A condition that cannot be faithfully reduced to bounded expression need not enter SILT evaluation.
- **Multiple Profile Expressions may coexist or conflict.** SILT supplies no universal precedence, merger or aggregate rule unless an expressed basis provides one.
- **Collective Standing does not imply representational Authority.** Belonging is distinct from power to bind, speak for, disclose for or commit a collective.
- **Technical Capability is distinct from Authority.** Technical success, key possession, credential validity, account state or capability possession do not by themselves establish semantic legitimacy.
- **Semantic continuity is distinct from cryptographic continuity.** Changes in keys, credentials or capability machinery do not automatically create or extinguish the underlying semantic relation.
- **Binding remains downstream.** Evaluation, Attribution and successful execution do not universally establish liability, enforceability, settlement or compulsory recognition.
- **No universal `Status` or `Capacity` object exists in v0.2.**
- Authentication, credentials, keys, capabilities, sessions, dynamic authorisation, access control, policy execution and runtime execution remain operational machinery below the Presentation line architecturally.

## 3. Specific questions for the review

Please stress-test the candidate against these questions.

### A. Source, Standing and Presentation

1. Does the architecture keep **Source** as ground/provenance rather than silently reducing it to issuer, credential, document or verifier?
2. Is **Standing** sufficiently relational and Source-grounded, including where the receiving order did not originate it?
3. Does **Presentation** remain a bounded encounter act rather than becoming a portable credential or a mechanism that constitutes Standing by presentation alone?
4. Is the distinction between evaluation and genuinely **constitutive Action** clear enough?

### B. Profile Expression and plural orders

5. Does Profile Expression remain a bounded interpretive/evaluative envelope rather than a hidden universal legal ontology?
6. Is the authority/provenance of a Profile Expression treated adequately, particularly for collective or contested orders?
7. Can multiple valid but conflicting Profile Expressions remain explicit without an unstated meta-order?
8. Does the deliberate non-expression rule avoid both forced translation and the opposite error of turning silence into proof of irrelevance or absence?

### C. Authority and technical systems

9. Is **Authority** sufficiently distinct from Technical Capability, credential validity and runtime success?
10. Does recursive / derived Authority preserve semantic lineage without requiring every downstream Action to reproduce the entire upstream Standing chain?
11. Are Revocation and validity dependencies expressed without assuming a universal cascade rule?
12. Is **Attribution** kept distinct from factual execution and from downstream Binding/liability?

### D. Obligation and private ordering

13. Does **Obligation** work as a persistent relational state that may survive changes in Standing, holder-related position or Authority?
14. In WE01, does the architecture successfully avoid collapsing technical control, possession/evidence and instrument-defined relational Standing?
15. Does the private-ordering encounter expose a missing universal concept, or is the current treatment through Source, Standing, Action, Authority, Obligation and downstream effect sufficient?

### E. Collective / Indigenous / customary pressure

16. Does the architecture accidentally require a collective or customary order to translate itself into the receiving system's ontology in order to participate?
17. Does it avoid assuming that a collective speaks with one voice or that individual membership implies collective Authority?
18. Is the non-compulsion / bounded-expression safeguard strong enough to prevent SILT from becoming a mechanism of ontological capture while still allowing meaningful evaluation at the boundary?

### F. Release claims

19. Does the draft release note accurately state what the four pressure tests establish, without converting them into proof that the architecture is universally complete?
20. Is anything in the README, changelog, migration record, conformance material or release note materially stronger than the canonical Core can support?

## 5. What is outside this review

Unless it reveals a release-blocking semantic defect, the following should be recorded as future or separate work rather than used to reopen v0.2:

- choosing a production serialisation or API;
- selecting DID/VC, capability, agent or authentication substrates;
- production validator design;
- universal trust-registry interoperability;
- certification and assurance machinery;
- full contributor / patent / SEP / trademark governance;
- redesigning the protected AUT CISRC / Vietsch v0.1 implementation as native v0.2;
- completing the ecosystem comparison matrix;
- adding further use cases merely for coverage;
- philosophical expansion that belongs in companion research or the book rather than Core.

## 6. Finding severity

Please classify findings as one of the following.

**BLOCKER — semantic defect**  
A contradiction, missing distinction or structural problem that makes the v0.2 architecture unsafe or materially incoherent. A BLOCKER triggers an FC2 correction before release.

**MAJOR — release claim / normative ambiguity**  
The architecture may stand, but wording in Core or public release material could reasonably produce a materially wrong implementation or overclaim. Must be corrected before release, but does not necessarily reopen the architecture.

**MINOR — editorial / explanatory**  
Clarity, wording, cross-reference or packaging improvement that does not alter semantic meaning. Fix if useful before release.

**FUTURE — valid but non-blocking work**  
Research, implementation, governance or v0.3 work that should be preserved without expanding the v0.2 release gate.

## 7. Requested response format

For each finding, please provide:

- **Severity:** BLOCKER / MAJOR / MINOR / FUTURE
- **Location:** section, invariant or worked encounter
- **Finding:** the issue in one or two sentences
- **Why it matters:** the semantic or release consequence
- **Minimal correction:** if one is available

Please also finish with one of these overall dispositions:

- **READY FOR v0.2.0** — no release-blocking semantic defect identified;
- **READY SUBJECT TO EDITORIAL CORRECTIONS** — no Core reopening required;
- **FC2 REQUIRED** — one or more semantic defects should be corrected and re-reviewed before release.

## 8. Reviewer note

The most useful review is adversarial rather than sympathetic. Please do not assume a concept works because its intent is attractive. In particular, try to break the architecture at the seams where different sources of legitimacy meet: collective authority, private ordering, institutional delegation, recursive agents, contradictory Profile Expressions and conditions that should not be translated at all.

At the same time, please distinguish a genuine semantic defect from a preference for a larger identity, governance or authorisation stack. SILT Core is intentionally narrower than those systems.

The release question is therefore not whether SILT solves every surrounding problem. It is whether the v0.2 grammar preserves the semantic distinctions it claims to preserve at the encounter.
