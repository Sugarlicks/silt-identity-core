# SILT Core - Interim Specification IPR Policy

**Status:** Interim publication policy for SILT Core v0.2. This is not yet a full standards-body contributor patent agreement.

## 1. Objective

SILT Core is intended to remain openly implementable semantic infrastructure. The project direction is royalty-free implementability of the public specification while preserving separate rights in trademarks, certification and commercial implementation services.

This interim policy makes the present boundary explicit rather than implying a patent commitment that has not yet been formally constituted.

## 2. Copyright and software licences

The normative specification and narrative public documentation are licensed under **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)**.

Reference code and machine-readable implementation, conformance and binding artefacts are licensed under **Apache License 2.0**, unless a file states otherwise.

CC BY 4.0 does not itself grant patent or trademark rights. Apache License 2.0 contains a patent grant from contributors for covered software contributions according to its terms; that grant must not be treated as a blanket patent licence for every implementation of the SILT specification.

## 3. Current patent position

SILT Core v0.2 does **not** represent that:

- no patents could read on an implementation;
- every contributor has made a patent non-assertion promise;
- the project has identified every potentially essential claim; or
- CC BY 4.0 supplies a standards patent licence.

No additional patent licence or non-assertion covenant should be inferred from publication of the v0.2 specification.

## 4. Interim contribution rule

The project will not knowingly incorporate a normative requirement that depends on a disclosed royalty-bearing patent claim where doing so would undermine the stated goal of royalty-free implementability.

For this interim policy, a **potential Essential Claim** means a patent claim that a contributor reasonably believes may be necessarily infringed by implementing a normative requirement of the SILT specification.

A person or organisation proposing a normative contribution SHOULD disclose any known potential Essential Claim that it or its represented organisation controls and that is materially relevant to the proposed requirement.

Where such a claim is identified, maintainers may:

1. redesign the requirement to avoid the claim;
2. require an adequate royalty-free licence or non-assertion commitment before merging the contribution;
3. keep the material non-normative; or
4. reject or defer the contribution.

## 5. No implication from participation

Discussion, issue comments, review participation or implementation feedback do not by themselves create a patent licence, copyright assignment or trademark licence beyond rights expressly granted in a submitted contribution.

Substantive normative contributions may be deferred until the contributor's rights position is sufficiently clear.

## 6. Direction for the formal policy

Before SILT enters a mature multi-contributor standards process, the interim policy should be replaced or supplemented by a formal royalty-free or non-assertion regime covering Essential Claims and contribution rights. The mechanism may be a contributor agreement, participation agreement or stewardship-body policy.

The design target is comparable in objective, though not yet in legal effect, to royalty-free standards regimes used by established standards organisations. The project must not claim equivalence until the relevant commitments actually exist.

## 7. Trademarks and certification

Patent and copyright licensing does not grant rights to SILT names, logos, certification marks or endorsement. See `TRADEMARK_AND_CONFORMANCE.md`.

## 8. No warranty

The applicable public licences contain their own warranty and liability terms. This interim policy does not provide a legal opinion, freedom-to-operate assessment or warranty of non-infringement.

## 9. Reference models reviewed

The policy direction has been informed by public royalty-free/non-assertion approaches used by W3C, OASIS and the OpenID Foundation. Those organisations' policies do not apply to SILT and are not incorporated by reference.

- W3C Patent Policy: https://www.w3.org/policies/patent-policy/
- OASIS IPR Policy / TC Handbook: https://docs.oasis-open.org/TChandbook/Reference/IPR.html
- OpenID Foundation IPR Policy: https://openid.net/intellectual-property/
